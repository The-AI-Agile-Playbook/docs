Refinement & Story Quality

3.1 INVEST Story Quality Analyzer

Stress-test user stories against the INVEST criteria before they enter the sprint. Identifies missing acceptance criteria, edge cases, and dependencies.

Prompt 
Act as a Senior Business Analyst and QA Architect. Analyze this user story against the INVEST criteria and provide a quality report.

User Story:
- Title: [Title]
- Description: [As a... I want... So that...]
- Acceptance Criteria: [List current criteria]
- Story Points (if estimated): [X]

Provide:
1. **INVEST Score** — Rate each criterion 1-5 and explain gaps.
   - Independent: Can this be delivered without other stories?
   - Negotiable: Is the solution prescribed or open?
   - Valuable: Is the business value explicit?
   - Estimable: Is there enough information to estimate?
   - Small: Can this be completed in one sprint?
   - Testable: Can QA write test cases from the acceptance criteria?

2. **Missing Acceptance Criteria** — List 3-5 criteria that are implied but not written.

3. **Edge Cases** — Identify 3 scenarios the team likely has not considered.

4. **Dependencies** — Flag any external dependencies or assumptions.

5. **Suggested Rewrite** — Rewrite the story with improved clarity, keeping the original intent intact.

3.2 User Story Generator from Raw Requirements

Transform vague stakeholder requirements into well-structured user stories with acceptance criteria ready for refinement.

Prompt 
Act as a Product Owner with deep technical understanding and 10 years of experience writing user stories for software teams.

Raw requirement from the stakeholder:
"[Paste the raw requirement, email, or meeting note here]"

Product context:
- Product name: [Name]
- User types: [List the main user personas]
- Technical constraints: [Any known constraints]

Transform this into:
1. **Decomposed User Stories** — Break the requirement into 2-5 independent user stories, each deliverable in one sprint.
2. For each story provide:
   - Title
   - Description (As a [persona], I want [action], so that [value])
   - Acceptance Criteria (Given/When/Then format, minimum 3 per story)
   - Story Points estimate (use Fibonacci: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8)
   - Dependencies on other stories
3. **Suggested Refinement Questions** — 3 questions the team should ask the stakeholder before committing.
4. **Out of Scope** — What this requirement does NOT include (prevent scope creep).

Prioritize clarity over creativity. The team should be able to start work immediately.

3.3 Technical Debt Story Creator

Frame technical debt items as business-value stories that POs and stakeholders can understand and prioritize.

Prompt 
Act as a Senior Software Architect who is also skilled at communicating technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders.

Technical debt item:
- Description: [What the tech debt is — e.g., "legacy authentication module with no tests"]
- Risk if ignored: [What could happen — e.g., "security vulnerability, slow feature development"]
- Estimated effort to fix: [e.g., "2-3 sprints of dedicated work"]
- Teams affected: [List]

Transform this into:
1. **Business-Framed User Story** — Write it so a non-technical PO understands the value. Focus on risk reduction, speed improvement, or cost avoidance.
2. **Cost of Delay** — Estimate what happens per month if this is NOT addressed (in hours lost, incidents expected, or features delayed).
3. **Incremental Plan** — Break the work into 3-5 thin vertical slices that each deliver standalone value (not "Phase 1: research, Phase 2: design" — each slice must be deployable).
4. **Elevator Pitch** — A 30-second explanation for the PO: "If we spend [X effort], we avoid [Y risk] and unlock [Z capability]."

No jargon in the business-facing outputs. Speak in dollars, hours, and risks.

Retrospectives

4.1 Ceremony Design Generator (PCFTR Framework)

Generate a fresh, targeted ceremony design based on your team's emotional state and specific challenges. Uses the Persona-Context-Task-Format-Restraints framework.

Prompt 
Act as [Persona: e.g., Team Psychology Expert and Master Facilitator with 20 years of experience in high-performance software teams].

Context: My team of [X people] is currently [emotional state: e.g., exhausted and cynical] due to [specific problem: e.g., 3 consecutive sprints of changing scope from stakeholders].

Task: Design a complete [ceremony type: e.g., Retrospective] agenda of [duration: e.g., 45 minutes] using the metaphor of [metaphor: e.g., a hospital emergency room] to surface hidden problems in a psychologically safe way.

Format:
- Minute-by-minute agenda
- Exact facilitator instructions for each segment
- 3 specific questions to ask during each segment
- A method for anonymous input collection
- A closing action that produces a concrete, assignable outcome

Restraints:
- Do NOT use Start/Stop/Continue or any format my team has seen before.
- Do NOT include icebreakers longer than 3 minutes.
- Every segment must produce a written artifact.

- Exact text to paste in chat for each transition
- How to handle silence (specific recovery moves)
- Closing ritual that creates shared ownership

Sprint Planning & Capacity

5.1 Evidence-Based Capacity Forecasting

Generate a data-backed capacity recommendation using historical sprint data. Stop overcommitting — let the numbers fight the battle.

Prompt 
Act as an Agile Forecasting Analyst. Based on my team's historical sprint data, provide a capacity recommendation for next sprint.

Historical Data:
[Sprint -1: Committed X, Completed Y, Carryover Z]
[Sprint -2: Committed X, Completed Y, Carryover Z]
[Sprint -3: Committed X, Completed Y, Carryover Z]
[Sprint -4: Committed X, Completed Y, Carryover Z]
[Sprint -5: Committed X, Completed Y, Carryover Z]

Team context for next sprint:
- Team members available: [X of Y] (note any absences)
- Known tech debt items that must be addressed: [list]
- External dependencies or risks: [list]
- Sprint Goal: [state the goal]

Provide:
1. **Recommended capacity** in story points (use the median of completed points, not the average — averages are skewed by outliers).
2. **Confidence interval** — "I am 80% confident you can complete between X and Y points."
3. **Risk factors** that could reduce capacity (absences, holidays, dependencies).
4. **Buffer recommendation** — How much capacity to reserve for unplanned work based on historical disruption rate.
5. **One sentence** I can read to the team: "Based on our data, we should commit to no more than X points this sprint."

Do NOT inflate the number. Underpromise, overdeliver.

Prompt

[Story 1: Title — brief description]
[Story 2: Title — brief description]
[Story 3: Title — brief description]
[Story 4: Title — brief description]
[Story 5: Title — brief description]

Sprint Review

6.1 Decision-Driven Sprint Review Narrative

Prepare a Sprint Review narrative focused on business outcomes and stakeholder decisions — not a feature demo script.

Prompt 
Act as an Agile Communication Specialist. Help me prepare a Sprint Review narrative that focuses on business outcomes, not technical details.

Sprint Data:
- Sprint Goal: [Goal]
- Goal Achieved: [Yes/No/Partially]
- Stories Completed: [List with brief descriptions]
- Stories Not Completed: [List with reasons]
- Key Metrics: [Any relevant data — user engagement, performance, error rates]
- Stakeholder Concerns from Last Review: [List any open items]

Generate:
1. **Opening Statement** (30 seconds) — Frame the sprint in terms of business impact, not features shipped.
2. **Demo Script** — For each completed story, write a 2-sentence description focused on user/business value (not technical implementation).
3. **Feedback Questions** — For each demo item, write one specific question to ask stakeholders (not "What do you think?" but "Does this solve the problem of [specific issue]?").
4. **Decision Points** — List 2-3 decisions the stakeholders need to make during this meeting.
5. **Closing Summary Template** — A fill-in-the-blank summary: "This sprint we [achieved/partially achieved] our goal of [goal]. The key decisions made are: [1, 2, 3]. The backlog will be updated to reflect [changes]."

[Week 1: X days, Week 2: X days, ...]

Deployment Frequency:
[Week 1: X deployments, Week 2: X deployments, ...]

Change Failure Rate:
[Week 1: X%, Week 2: X%, ...]

[Week 1: X hours, Week 2: X hours, ...]

Additional context:
- Team size: [X]

5. **Management Translation** — Write a 3-sentence summary I can present to my VP that frames these metrics in business terms (cost of delay, time to market, reliability).

Do not sugarcoat the results. Give me the truth.

8.1 Team Health & Burnout Detection

Analyze pulse survey data and team signals to detect burnout, isolation, and disengagement before they escalate.

Prompt 
Act as an Organizational Psychologist specializing in software team wellbeing and burnout prevention. I am a Scrum Master monitoring my team's health.

Pulse Survey Data (last [X] weeks):
[Team Member A: Energy [5,4,3,2,2], Support [4,4,3,3,2], Words: "good, okay, tired, stressed, exhausted"]
[Team Member B: Energy [3,3,3,3,3], Support [2,2,2,2,2], Words: "fine, fine, okay, fine, whatever"]
[Team Member C: Energy [4,4,5,4,4], Support [5,5,5,4,5], Words: "great, productive, energized, busy, solid"]

Additional signals (if available):
- Meeting participation patterns: [describe any changes]
- Async communication frequency: [increasing/stable/decreasing]
- PR review turnaround trends: [any changes per individual]
- Recent team/org changes: [describe any relevant events]

Analyze and provide:
1. **Individual Risk Assessment** — Flag any team member showing signs of burnout, disengagement, or isolation. Use a traffic light system (Green/Yellow/Red).
2. **Trend Analysis** — Identify declining trends even if current scores are still acceptable.
3. **Root Cause Hypotheses** — For each Yellow/Red flag, suggest possible causes based on the data patterns.
4. **Intervention Recommendations** — Specific actions I can take THIS WEEK for each at-risk individual (1:1 conversation starters, workload adjustments, connection opportunities).
5. **Team-Level Health Score** — Overall team health rating with a comparison to previous period.

Prioritize early detection. I would rather have a false alarm than miss a real problem.
